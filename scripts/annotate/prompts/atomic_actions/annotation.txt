# Annotate atomic actions, physical results, and failure/non-failure

Label the entire episode relative to its language task. Use only the supplied
synchronized camera images and ordinary robot telemetry. Judge this episode from
its own evidence. Do not assume either success or failure in advance.

Separate three judgments:
- Action: what operation the robot performs or attempts.
- Observed result: what physically changes, remains unchanged, or is unresolved.
- Failure: whether an independently supported failure occurs during the behavior.

## Physical outcome for every action

For every action, explicitly judge the concrete physical result separately from
task correctness and uncertainty. Use an affirmative or negative statement about
the result relevant to that operation, rather than leaving the description at
"attempts", "appears", or generic "success/failure". Evaluate the result by operation:
- reach/approach: the gripper reaches the interaction position, or has not reached it.
- grasp: the robot establishes control of the object, or does not acquire it.
- lifting motion: the object rises under robot control, or the tool rises without it.
- transport: the object relocates under robot control, or that relocation does not occur.
- release: the object separates from the gripper, or remains retained.
- withdraw: the gripper moves away from the interaction, or remains there.
Preserve the canonical action vocabulary; lifting motion is a physical result,
not an additional required action category. For idle, state the observed
absence of substantive motion/interaction rather than inventing a completion goal.

Judge the physical result supported by the segment's evidence, including its
supported outcome by the end. Initial uncertainty must not replace an outcome
that becomes established within the segment. Uncertainty about the exact onset
does not make an established outcome unresolved. Conversely, an outcome supported
only after the segment does not establish that it occurred within this interval.
An ongoing action can have a result not yet achieved; that alone is not failure.
State material transitions or later loss of a result rather than implying the
outcome held throughout every frame.

Treat each physical transition and resulting state as a separate evidence claim.
An observed transition does not establish its expected downstream state. Distinguish
separation, entry into a destination, contact with a surface, and stable support
or settling. Visible location inside a destination does not by itself establish
support, and downward movement does not establish that settling has finished.
Claim stable or retained support only when the supplied observations establish
support and persistence of that state, rather than continued transit or falling.
Do not infer an unobserved end state from the intended action, typical physics,
successful separation, or the recording endpoint.

When the recording ends before a resulting state can be verified, describe only
the last supported transition/state and explicitly identify the unobserved result
as not shown or not verified. Keep established results affirmative; uncertainty
about a downstream state must not erase an independently established transition.
Set review_required=true and explain which result lacks confirming evidence.
Missing confirmation of a later state alone does not establish failure.

When evidence cannot verify the physical outcome, state the best-supported
physical judgment as provisional, set review_required=true, and explain the
missing/conflicting evidence in uncertainty. Absence of evidence is not a verified
negative. Do not turn this reporting requirement into an unsupported claim of
certainty. Keep what_happened and observed_result consistent about the same result.
Task mismatch does not erase an achieved physical result: acquiring, lifting, or
relocating the wrong object can physically succeed while being semantic failure.

For task mismatch, distinguish target evidence from physical-result evidence.
An object-directed grasp attempt can be visibly incorrect before successful
control is established. Occluded finger/contact surfaces do not by themselves
make the target unknown: reconcile object location, finger alignment, closure,
and continuous tracking across both views. Use the target supported by that
combined evidence, rather than requiring visible contact surfaces or lifting.

Localize semantic failure to the first supported interaction with an incorrect
referent, independently of whether physical acquisition succeeds. If target
engagement remains ambiguous across the combined views, keep the target judgment
provisional until distinguishing evidence is available. Later acquisition alone
does not establish an earlier interaction that was not visible.

Before marking a named wrong-object grasp attempt non-failure, check whether the
uncertainty is about the TARGET or only the RESULT. Uncertain acquisition alone
does not cancel a supported task mismatch. If target engagement is genuinely
unresolved, explain the competing referents and the missing distinguishing cue.

## Temporal target: observable failure, not causal responsibility

Localize the interval where a failure is observable in the supplied evidence.
We are NOT assigning causal responsibility, finding the earlier action/frame that
caused a later failure, or estimating the hidden physical onset of an event.
start_frame for a failure segment means the first supplied observation that
supports the observable failing behavior/state. It does not mean when the robot
made the responsible decision or when the failed attempt began.

Keep action timing separate from failure timing. Reaching, closure, opening, or
another operation may begin before any failure is observable. A later failure
does not make the entire attempt a failure. Split a continuing action when failure
first becomes observably supported; retain its action_id across that label change.
Do not backdate failure to an action start, command, possible initiating contact,
or guessed responsible frame merely because the subsequent outcome is bad.

Disengagement initiation does not establish release failure. Label delayed
release only once the images/relative motion support
abnormal continued retention or another observable failure to disengage. Initial
opening with unresolved separation must not be labeled failure solely because
delayed separation becomes visible later. This rule applies to every task and
episode; do not impose a fixed number of frames of acceptable opening latency.

Later observations may clarify object identities or confirm interpretation, but
each localized positive failure needs evidence of failing behavior/state at that
time. A later bad outcome alone is insufficient. Cite the supplied frame(s) and
views that support the observable failure and explain the relevant change.

With sparse images, use the first failure-supporting supplied observation as the
positive onset; report the interval since the preceding sample in uncertainty.
Do not move the positive onset earlier within that bracket to guess when the
hidden event actually began. Earlier unresolved intervals remain provisional;
lack of observable failure is not proof of a verified negative.

End a failure label when its failing behavior/state observably ceases, or when
the failing operation ends and no continuing failure is independently supported.
An action change alone does not prove a failure began or ended. Do not assign
later segments failure merely because the episode failed or an earlier failure's
consequences remain. Flag uncertain cessation instead of claiming an exact hidden
contact/disengagement time.

A physically successful operation can be task-inconsistent. Assess physical
outcome and semantic correctness independently from their supporting evidence.

## Object identity and evidence

Separate the requested object from the object the robot actually interacts with.
The task specifies the goal; it does not identify whatever is inside the gripper.
Track appearance and location across both cameras, before, during, and after
interaction. Use readable markings and distinguishing features; quote packaging
text only when legible. Never complete blurry lettering from expectations. Color
alone may not distinguish objects. Reconcile conflicting views.

Report supported match, supported mismatch, or unresolved, with source frames and
camera views. Failure to locate the requested object alone does not prove mismatch.
A clearly identified wrong object can establish mismatch without locating the
requested object elsewhere. Do not invent hidden perception or planning causes.

Use the instruction's object/destination terms as the canonical names whenever
the observed referents are supported as matching them. Do not substitute a
packaging or physical-form name for a supported matching instruction term.
Describe packaging shape/material as additional properties when relevant,
without replacing the canonical referent name. Apply this rule to destinations
and fixtures as well as interaction objects.

Create stable identifiers from these canonical names, using lowercase words
joined by underscores and a numerical suffix.
Different physical instances receive different suffixes. When a manipulated
object does not match the instruction, use its independently supported actual
name and explicitly describe the mismatch. Never assign the requested name to an
incorrect or unresolved referent solely because it appears in the instruction.
For unresolved identities, use the generic prefix object or destination with a
numerical suffix and explain the uncertainty; do not invent a product name.

Determine each referent's canonical name from the available identity evidence
for the episode and assign its identifier consistently across all segments.
Later identity confirmation may name an already continuously tracked referent;
it does not establish earlier target engagement, possession, or failure timing.
If identity remains conflicting, retain a generic identifier and flag review.
Reuse an existing identifier if a destination is also interacted with as an
object. Never merge distinct objects because they look similar. Identifiers are
local to the episode and do not prove correspondence across model annotations.
Record appearance, packaging, product identity, and source evidence in identity_check.
Use null when no specific referent can be supported; explain material uncertainty.

For apparent grasp, loss, or release, compare before/after relative motion in both
cameras. Commands, finger aperture, proximity, or tool movement alone do not prove
contact, possession, or separation. Occlusion does not prove dropping. Remaining
near the tool does not prove attachment. Distinguish carriage from surface support.

## Failure and non-failure

Assign one binary label to every segment: failure=true or failure=false.
Non-failure groups useful progress, successful behavior, neutral behavior, and
ordinary pauses together. Do not assign a separate progress or neutral label.
Non-failure does not imply that the instructed task is complete or successful.

Failure means an observable unsuccessful attempt, task-inconsistent action,
harmful side effect, or sustained unproductive behavior. Lack of immediate progress
alone is not failure. A brief pause or recording endpoint alone does not establish
stalling, timeout, or sudden termination. Do not invent hardware faults or damage.

If a behavior advances the task while also causing an independently supported
failure, label it failure=true and record the supported failure categories.
Useful progress or eventual success does not cancel a concurrent failure.
Failure=false means no failure is sufficiently supported for that interval;
material uncertainty must still be recorded and is not a verified negative.

A later wrong-object interaction does not retroactively make an earlier
non-failing reach a failure. Start semantic failure when incorrect targeting or
behavior first becomes observably supported, and record boundary uncertainty.

Wrong-object engagement and successful acquisition are separate judgments.
Label semantic failure when the robot visibly directs an interaction at an
identifiable incorrect object, even if acquisition is unresolved or unsuccessful.
Do not require lifting, carriage, or established control to support that task
error. Incorrect target engagement can support semantic failure independently
of the physical grasp result. Closure alone, proximity, or approach into an
ambiguous object cluster does not
identify which object is engaged. Explain the frame/view evidence distinguishing
the incorrect target from neighboring objects. Later views may clarify the
tracked object's identity, but cannot establish an earlier interaction that was
not visible. If target engagement is unresolved, flag that uncertainty rather
than backdating from later acquisition. Assess physical grasp failure separately.

A successful episode may contain failures. A semantic error does not make later
physical behavior irrelevant. Continue checking for missed attempts, control loss,
release problems, collisions, and other side effects through the final image.

## Canonical action vocabulary

Use these terms or their grammatical forms when supported. Actions include
attempts; the action term alone never asserts successful completion.

reach
- Meaning: move the gripper into position for an interaction, including local alignment.
- Start: the first supported directed movement toward that interaction position.
- End: the interaction begins, the target changes, or the robot withdraws/abandons it.
- Synonyms: approach; move toward; align before interaction.
- Evidence requirement: movement must be directed toward a supported interaction referent.

grasp
- Meaning: attempt to establish control of an object.
- Start: supported engagement or closure directed at acquiring control.
- End: control is established, the attempt is abandoned, or a distinct retry begins.
- Synonyms: acquire; take hold; pick up when describing acquisition of control.
- Evidence requirement: successful acquisition requires supported control of an object.
- Initial lifting can provide evidence of control; subsequent controlled relocation
  is transport. Do not insert a separate lift merely for a small verification motion.

transport
- Meaning: relocate an object under established control, including destination positioning.
- Start: controlled relocation begins after acquisition of control.
- End: destination positioning ends, control is lost, or another operation begins.
- Synonyms: carry; move a held object; relocate a held object.
- Evidence requirement: transport requires supported object relocation under control.
- If control is materially unresolved, state that transport is apparent/provisional.

release
- Meaning: attempt to relinquish control of an object.
- Start: supported disengagement behavior begins.
- End: separation is established, the attempt is abandoned, or another operation begins.
- Synonyms: let go; disengage from a held object.
- Evidence requirement: successful release requires supported physical separation.

withdraw
- Meaning: move away from an interaction.
- Start: supported movement away from the interaction begins.
- End: withdrawal ends or another interaction begins.
- Synonyms: retract; back away.
- Classify directed movement toward a new interaction as reach; increased distance
  from the previous interaction alone does not establish withdrawal.

idle
- Meaning: no substantive interaction or relocation is occurring.
- Start: substantive activity ends without a new operation beginning.
- End: substantive activity resumes.
- Synonyms: pause; remain stationary; hold position without a new operation.
- Segment idle only when substantive operation has ceased; keep incidental pauses
  within the continuing operation.
- Idle is not automatically failure or task abandonment.

Use a task-specific operation when it describes the behavior more precisely than
the core vocabulary. Name the affected object or part
and the supported before/after change. This vocabulary is not exhaustive: use an
appropriate ordinary verb and explain it when the core terms do not fit. Use
unknown only when the operation cannot be resolved, and require review.

Keep physical result terms separate from actions: control established, missed,
slipped, dropped, released, displaced, blocked, unresolved. Use them only when
supported. An unsuccessful or delayed release is still the action release;
describe its failed, delayed, or unresolved result explicitly. A slip or drop is
a physical event, not an intentional robot operation unless evidence supports that.

Recovery describes a task effect; identify the physical action that attempts or
achieves it. Never turn an attempted recovery into an established successful recovery.

## Atomic action boundaries and localized task effects

An atomic action contains one continuous operation on the same object or
destination within the same attempt. End it when the operation completes, is
interrupted, is abandoned, or changes. Atomic means an observable reusable operation,
not every joint movement or every verb used in a description.

Start a new atomic action when the operation, interacted object, destination, or
distinct attempt changes. Keep local alignment corrections and tiny pauses within
one continuous operation. A new attempt requires an observable reset or renewed
engagement after completion, interruption, or abandonment. Repeated finger motion
alone does not establish multiple attempts. Do not split simply to increase count.

Give each atomic action an action_id with the prefix a and a consecutive integer
suffix starting at 1, in chronological order.
Grasp and transport remain different actions even if their failure labels
match. Do not merge different actions solely because their binary labels match.

Within an action, split annotation segments when failure or the supported
failure-category set changes. These pieces retain the same action_id while the
operation continues. Localize each category to its supported interval within the
action; do not spread a temporary failure category across the entire operation.
If the operation itself is interrupted, assign a new action_id to subsequent behavior.

Merge adjacent pieces only when they belong to the same uninterrupted action and
have the same failure label and category set. A distinct reset/retry receives
a new action_id even if its verb and task labels match the preceding attempt.
Each action_id occurs in one contiguous block and refers to one operation/attempt.

Give an evidence-based reason for the start of every segment. Use episode_start
for the first segment; otherwise explain the operation/participant/attempt change
or local failure transition. Changes in wording or progress alone do not justify a boundary.

Use consecutive half-open source-frame intervals [start_frame, end_frame), covering
[0, n_frames) exactly once, including pauses: no gaps, overlaps, or empty intervals.
The first start is 0, each start equals the previous end, and the final end is n_frames.
Place action boundaries at supported operation transitions. Failure/category
onsets follow the observable-failure rule above, independently of action starts.
Images may be sparse or irregular: explain bracketing source frames in uncertainty;
action-transition estimates must not backdate positive failure to a hidden event.
Do not imply exact physical timing. Never cite n_frames as an
image. Cite only supplied image frames/views. Commands at t follow observation t.

## Failure categories

Choose all independently supported categories for each failure segment:
- reaching: an unsuccessful pre-contact reach to the interaction object/location.
  A barely missed grasp belongs to grasp instead.
- grasp: failed acquisition or physical grasp/control problems, including an
  incorrect physical grasp, slipping, dropping, or loss of control.
- manipulation: established control but unsuccessful post-contact manipulation
  or disengagement of the interacted object/fixture.
  Localize this label to observable failed manipulation; do not apply it from the
  start of an attempt solely because failure is observed later.
- sequencing_semantic: wrong object, action, destination, or order. Describe the
  observed task inconsistency without claiming a hidden planning defect.
- collision: supported unwanted physical contact by the robot, gripper, or carried
  object with another object, fixture, or the environment. Wanted task contact
  includes grasping the interaction object and supporting a placed object inside
  its destination. Unwanted contact includes striking a rim or nearby object,
  or pushing, lifting, tilting, dragging, or obstructing the placement container
  through contact during placement. Contact that disturbs the container in these
  ways counts as collision even when placement eventually succeeds. Damage,
  lasting disruption, and obstruction are not required. Judge unwanted contact
  by these observable interaction criteria, without inferring the robot's intent.
  Support may be directly visible contact or a physical
  reaction in a stable view that clearly links the robot or carried object's
  interaction to the disturbance. For indirect evidence, cite the before/after
  frames and explain what links the interaction to the disturbance; the exact
  contacting part need not be visible if that causal link is supported.
  Motion alone does not prove contact or its cause. Account for camera motion,
  especially in the moving wrist view; a change in apparent position or tilt in
  that view alone is insufficient. Explain which unwanted-contact criterion the
  evidence supports. An occluded contacting part alone does not make an otherwise
  supported disturbance unresolved. If contact, its cause, or whether it meets
  the unwanted-contact criteria remains unresolved, omit
  collision from categories, set review_required=true, and describe the possible
  collision, its approximate interval, supporting cues, and unresolved fact in
  uncertainty. Omission means not sufficiently supported, not confirmed absence.
  Keep independently supported categories. Do not assert uncertain contact as
  fact in observed_result or what_happened, or create a segment boundary solely
  for a suspected collision without a supported action/task-label transition.
  Start the positive collision label at observable unwanted contact/disturbance,
  not at the earlier approach or inferred initiating force. A stationary container
  does not by itself prove contact has ceased; explain material end uncertainty.
- task_not_attempted: supported absence of a discernible task attempt or sustained
  stalling; do not infer this from an ordinary pause or brief lack of progress.
- sudden_episode_termination: supported abnormal interruption or cutoff, not merely
  the final frame of a recording.
- visible_hardware_issue: an independently supported visible mechanical malfunction;
  do not infer a fault from unexplained motion or inactivity alone.
- other: a supported failure outside these definitions; explain specifically.

These are failure categories, not action-stage labels. Successful reach/grasp/
transport/release on the wrong object is semantic failure, not automatically a
physical failure. Add physical categories only for independent failures meeting
their definitions. A physical failure on the wrong object can co-occur with the
semantic failure. Record the complete supported category set locally.

For failure segments, suggest a recovery separately from the observed facts.
Severity: low = prevents this attempt without meaningful damage/risk; medium =
meaningful disruption, damage, or risk while the environment remains usable;
catastrophic = significant consequences preventing safe/practical use without
repair or replacement. Describe supported consequences; flag speculative risk.
Resetability: immediate = reattempt without intervention; minor_reset = small
straightforward intervention; hard_reset = substantial effort/intervention;
unresettable = repair/replacement beyond normal resetting is required.
Uncertain consequence judgments require review; do not invent damage or repairs.

## Standardized descriptions and uncertainty

Write what_happened using this pattern:
actor + canonical operation + object/destination + observed result + failure effect, if any.

Prefer the robot as the action subject. Use robot arm or robot gripper when the
part-specific motion/state matters. Preserve stable object/destination identifiers.
Use the stable identifier based on the instruction's term for a supported matching
referent, rather than alternating between the instruction term and packaging
names. Use an evidence-supported actual name for mismatches and a generic name
for unresolved identities, as specified above. Put detailed appearance and
identity evidence in identity_check. Do not substitute a pronoun when its referent
could be confused with another participant.

Use the canonical action verb consistently in both action and what_happened:
reach/reaches, grasp/grasps, transport/transports, release/attempts to release,
withdraw/withdraws, and idle/idles. The vocabulary's synonyms explain recognition;
use the canonical output verb consistently instead of substituting synonyms for
the same operation. Supported task-specific verbs remain allowed.
An attempt marker is required when successful acquisition or separation is not
established; use "grasps ... and establishes control" or "releases ... and
establishes separation" only when that result is supported.

Write short sentences in a stable order: operation and participants, then concrete
physical result, then the supported failure effect when applicable. Use these
result phrases when the corresponding fact is supported:
- alignment for grasp: "The gripper aligns around <object>". When the same
  surrounding alignment is supported, do not alternate this phrase with
  "reaches the interaction position around <object>" or "straddles <object>".
  Preserve a different spatial relation when supported; alignment does not prove
  contact or acquisition.
- acquisition: "establishes control of <object>".
- continued carriage: "<object> remains under gripper control".
- separation: "<object> separates from the gripper".
- retained support: "<object> remains supported inside/on <destination>";
  choose the observed spatial relation, preserving inside/on/outside distinctions.
  Use this phrase only when retained support is independently established;
  do not append it automatically after separation or destination entry.
- unresolved separation: record the evidence uncertainty separately; describe
  the best-supported physical judgment as provisional when it cannot be verified.
- delayed separation: "Separation is delayed"; specify observed continued
  object/gripper motion when supported, without inventing an attachment mechanism.
- environmental disturbance: "Contact displaces/lifts/tilts/drags/obstructs
  <object or fixture>"; choose only the supported effect or effects.
- wrong-object behavior: "<actual object> is not the requested <task object>";
  use only when object mismatch is supported.

For a reach, describe directed movement toward the supported referent followed
by the supported alignment result using the canonical result phrase. Use the
same alignment judgment as the physical result in observed_result; append
additional material motion or spatial facts only when supported. If alignment
has not yet occurred, state that the gripper has not yet aligned around the
object; if the result is uncertain, mark the best-supported judgment provisional.
Do not require every reach to achieve alignment or insert acquisition, contact,
or task completion into a reach.

Describe explicit object support, separation, location, and motion rather than
generic completion claims. For withdrawal after placement, describe withdrawal
separately from the object's retained support/location when those facts are
visible. Omit unsupported facts and retain different outcomes explicitly.

Keep observed_result and what_happened consistent about the physical result.
Do not add stock sentences merely to share keywords. Preserve additional material
facts, including further disturbance or its supported absence, rather than forcing
different observations into one phrase. Keep natural, concise descriptions;
do not output keyword lists or boilerplate.
Preserve attempts, negation, uncertainty, actor/object roles, concurrent effects,
and temporal order. Attempts to release must never be normalized into releases.

When an action, identity, task judgment, result, or boundary is materially uncertain,
set review_required=true and explain the missing/conflicting evidence. Assign the
best-supported failure boolean for coverage, but explicitly mark affected
judgments provisional. A provisional no is not a verified negative. Never default
to failure=false solely because evidence is missing. Identity uncertainty must not
erase an independently visible physical failure.

## Input and response format

The input supplies episode_id, task, n_frames, fps, sampled source-frame indices,
camera images labeled agent/wrist, and any ordinary robot telemetry. Use actual
source timestamps and supplied frame lists; do not assume a fixed sampling rate.

Return only an unfenced JSON object with exactly episode_id, identity_check, segments.

identity_check has exactly:
- requested_object: the task's requested object.
- manipulated_object: supported identity/appearance and stable identifiers of
  interacted objects; do not invent a specific product name.
- match_status: supported_match, supported_mismatch, or unresolved.
- evidence: a nonempty array of objects with exactly frame (supplied integer source
  index), camera (agent or wrist), observation (specific visible evidence).
- uncertainty: material identity uncertainty, or an empty string.

segments is a nonempty complete partition of [0, n_frames). Each segment has exactly:
- start_frame: inclusive integer; for a newly positive failure/category, the first
  supplied observation supporting that observable failure, not its inferred cause.
- end_frame: exclusive integer.
- action_id: chronological atomic action identifier; preserve it across local
  label-change splits of the same action.
- action: canonical operation, supported task-specific verb, or unknown.
- object_id: stable interacted-object/fixture identifier, or null if not applicable
  or unresolved. Explain material referent uncertainty.
- destination_id: stable destination identifier, or null if not applicable/unresolved.
- observed_result: concise observed physical result, or an explicit unresolved result.
- boundary_reason: evidence-based explanation of this segment's start.
- failure: JSON boolean.
- what_happened: canonical action, concrete physical result, and any supported
  failure effect, using the standardized wording above.
- categories: a nonempty subset of the failure-category identifiers above when
  failure=true; otherwise an empty array.
- recovery: suggested recovery when failure=true; otherwise null.
- severity: low, medium, or catastrophic when failure=true; otherwise null.
- resetability: immediate, minor_reset, hard_reset, or unresettable when failure=true;
  otherwise null.
- evidence: a nonempty array of frame/camera/observation objects as defined above;
  include before/after evidence needed to establish a transition.
- review_required: JSON boolean, true for materially uncertain judgments.
- uncertainty: explanation, affected judgments, and any boundary brackets when
  review_required=true; otherwise an empty string.

Before returning, check full coverage, atomic action boundaries, local failure
timing, supported evidence, stable identities, and consistency among action,
physical result, failure, and categories. Check all applicable failure
categories through the final image without forcing any failure to exist.
For every claimed resulting state, check that the cited evidence establishes that
state rather than merely its preceding transition or an expected later outcome.
Stop outcome claims at the last supplied observation; flag unverified end states.
For every positive failure/category onset, verify that cited observations support
failure at that time. Reject backdating based only on a later outcome, an earlier
action/command start, or attribution of responsibility.
